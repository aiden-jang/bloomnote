# Bloomnote UI

Bloomnote is botanical stationery: ivory paper, deep green ink and restrained blush accents.
The garden is the visual centerpiece, with the recipient's words kept distinct from generated
flower meanings. Fraunces carries the expressive headlines and notes; DM Sans carries controls.

The composer uses a two-column studio at 900px and above, capped at 1200px: a paper writing surface first and an open garden preview beside it, without matching rounded cards. Below that it becomes one column, with writing before the preview. On phones,
compact spacing and fields of at least 16px avoid focus zoom. The fresh garden preview is labeled
as an example; draft and generated previews are labeled as the person's garden.

Controls have quiet borders and consistent corners. Selected choices keep their labels and clear
contrast. The library uses two columns on wide screens and one on phones. Recipient notes get a
separate paper card; opening, secret notes, watering, replies and keepsakes retain their behavior.

Dry humor belongs in supporting copy, never user-written notes, errors, dates, privacy or account
controls. Garden growth remains the main motion; reduced-motion behavior and keyboard focus remain
available. UI captures use isolated fixtures, never production account or garden writes.

The phone composer keeps the first note field inside the opening viewport. Its labeled example
preview uses the existing scene geometry at a smaller scale; generated and recipient gardens
keep their full presentation. The library gives color-based bouquet previews a separate band,
with recipient names above a quieter action row. Shared wordmarks link back to the composer.

Growing feedback retains the real status and Cancel action with a short supporting caption.
Sharing is visually primary; regrowing and starting over sit together below a divider. Empty
libraries keep one clear creation action and a small sprout.

The finished composer leads with sending and copying before the recipient preview. Preview notes and flower meanings use quieter, flatter surfaces so the garden and sharing controls carry the hierarchy.

The garden library header carries a small botanical envelope. A pressed-flower bookmark accompanies the recipient’s keepsake disclosure. Both are static decorative vectors, separate from the person’s words and garden state; empty libraries retain their sprout and one creation action.
