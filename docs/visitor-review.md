# Visitor walkthrough

Use this with someone who has not tried Bloomnote. Ask them to think aloud before
explaining controls. Record what they expect, where they hesitate and whether
they understand what has been saved or sent. This is a prepared procedure, not a
completed visitor study.

## Write and share

1. Open the writing desk. Can they explain what the app does and whether an
   account is needed? Can they distinguish an example from their own note?
2. Write a short fictional note, choose an occasion and generate a garden in a
   local fixture. Real generation spends provider quota.
3. Ask whether the recipient has received anything yet. They should understand
   that generating saves a garden, while sharing its link is a separate action.
4. Copy the link or save the image. Try a refused clipboard or cancelled share
   sheet. Check that recovery is clear and the note is retained.

## Receive and keep

1. Open the fixture link as a recipient. Try a flower with mouse, touch and
   keyboard, read the note, reveal a secret and grow a response.
2. Ask whether a scheduled reveal protects the note. It is presentation; anyone
   with the link can access the stored garden.
3. Keep a flower and a local memory. Confirm the difference between device-only
   keepsakes and the optional synced account library.
4. Remove a library entry, Undo, and simulate a failed removal. The listing must
   recover, and the shared gift link must keep working.
5. Simulate an Accounts outage. The device library should stay available without
   claiming the account is empty or signed out.

## Physical devices and accessibility

On an actual phone, try the keyboard, long names, native sharing, image saving,
Back navigation and installed-app recovery. Test optional voice recording only
with the visitor's permission: decline permission, start, stop and reach the recording cap;
confirm the microphone indicator turns off and playback works after reopening.
Voice memories remain on that device and are not part of shared gardens.

Use VoiceOver or TalkBack to hear flower labels, reveal progress, errors, library
tabs and saved feedback. Inspect enlarged text and reduced motion. Browser
emulation, mocked media devices and automated tests do not establish these
physical-device or spoken-output results.
